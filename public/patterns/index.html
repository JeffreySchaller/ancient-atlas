Patterns

The same idea, in places that never met.

Seven engineering signatures, tracked across 618 sites. Open one to watch the studies that argue it.

No. 01 · Machining

Tool marks that repeat on four continents.

Striations, bore holes, saw kerfs and flat-planed faces that look mechanical. Four continents, no contact between them, and stone their documented toolkits were not supposed to cut.

No. 02 · Precision

Tolerances you would ask a machine shop for.

Joints you cannot slide paper into. Surfaces flat to a fraction of a millimetre, corners cut square in granite, and the tolerance holds across whole buildings rather than one showpiece block.

166 sites · 31 countries

No. 03 · Polygonal

No two blocks alike, and no mortar anywhere.

Many-sided stones cut to fit each other rather than to a standard. No vertical joint runs the height of the wall, so when the ground moves the wall flexes instead of failing.

84 sites · 21 countries

No. 04 · Geometry

The same shapes, drawn by people who never met.

Circles, spirals, cup-and-ring marks, precise alignments and repeated proportions. Every inhabited continent carries some of them, in cultures with no route between them.

177 sites · 37 countries

No. 05 · Scale

Stones no recorded method could move.

Blocks from 50 to 1,500 tons, quarried, dressed, and in most cases carried somewhere else, by people whose documented toolkit was rope, timber and muscle.

249 sites · 42 countries

No. 06 · Hardness

Stone harder than the tools that shaped it.

Granite, diorite, basalt and andesite, 6 to 7 on Mohs, worked to a finish by cultures whose surviving tools are copper and stone. Softer does not cut harder.

No. 07 · Stratigraphy

Ground that argues with the date on the sign.

Weathering, sediment and buried courses that read older than the date on the sign. The one criterion where the evidence sits under the building rather than in it.
