Sage Wall (Montana)

MegalithicNorth AmericaUnited States

Megalithic interlocking wall structure on a private ranch in the Tobacco Root Mountains, southwestern Montana. Discovered by the landowner family in the 1980s. Features polygonal joinery stylistically comparable to Sacsayhuamán in Peru and Cusco's walls — irregularly-shaped, multi-ton blocks fitted without mortar. Mainstream archaeology classifies it as natural rock formation. Independent researchers (Brien Foerster, JJ Ainsworth, Jimmy Bright) argue the joinery angles, scoop marks, and interlocks indicate intentional construction. One of North America's most-debated megalithic anomalies.

45.4750°, -111.7833°

Look Closer — this site carries open questions: polygonal, interlocking masonry; stones fitted without mortar, almost no gap; geometry beyond period explanation; tool marks suggesting advanced machining.
