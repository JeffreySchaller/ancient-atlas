# Gemini Design Brief — "The Painted Ones" Fair Isle Capsule

Capsule I of the Ancient Atlas merch line. Five Pleistocene species,
all documented in Paleolithic cave art, rendered as a Fair Isle
knitting pattern. One design family → tee, print, eventually knitwear.
Gerald art-directs; this brief produces his raw material.

**The job the customer hires this for:** signaling "I know this thing"
to the few who recognize it, invisible to everyone else. Heritage
craft at six feet, extinct megafauna at two. Quiet wins. Anything
that reads as a cute animal sweater fails the brief.

**The storyline (carries into all copy):** these are not dinosaurs.
Humans saw every one of these animals, painted them on cave walls,
and remember them. Mammoths still walked Wrangel Island while the
Great Pyramid was built. The design is the world's oldest art
tradition, continued in yarn.

## MASTER PROMPT (paste first)

You are designing a traditional Fair Isle knitting pattern for a
premium heritage-craft brand. I need motif strips, not finished
garments.

Render a horizontal Fair Isle knitting chart band as flat 2D pixel
art on a visible stitch grid. Each "pixel" is one knit stitch and
should read as a slightly rounded V-shaped stitch, as in a real
stranded-knit fabric photographed flat. The band must tile seamlessly
left-to-right (the leftmost and rightmost columns continue each other
perfectly).

Hard constraints, never break these:
- Maximum 2 colors per horizontal row (true Fair Isle discipline)
- Total palette of exactly 4 colors: near-black obsidian #0D0D12,
  champagne gold #C9A84C, warm amber #E8B960, soft ivory #F0EEE9
- Obsidian is the ground; motifs work in champagne/amber/ivory
- Band height approximately 40 stitches, width a repeat of 60-80
  stitches shown tiled 3 times
- Flat frontal view, no garment, no folds, no mannequin, no text,
  no watermark, no signature
- Style register: Shetland/Scandinavian heritage knitting meets
  Paleolithic cave painting. Solemn, archaic, museum-quality.
  NOT cute, NOT cartoon, NOT childlike. Think Chauvet cave outline
  economy: the fewest marks that capture the animal's essential mass
  and motion.

The animals walk in profile procession, all facing left, evenly
spaced, with small archaic filler motifs between them (dots, lozenges,
chevrons) in traditional Fair Isle manner.

First, confirm you understand, then wait for the band subject.

## BAND PROMPTS (one per follow-up message)

**Band 1 — Woolly Mammoth (the anchor):**
Subject: woolly mammoth procession. Massive domed head, shoulder hump,
sloping back, long curved crossing tusks, trunk reaching slightly
forward and down, suggestion of long coat as a fringe along the belly
line. The silhouette must be unmistakable at arm's length. Champagne
body, ivory tusks, amber eye dot.

**Band 2 — Megaloceros (the spectacle):**
Subject: Megaloceros giganteus (giant deer) procession. The antlers
are the design: enormous palmate antlers spreading wide, nearly
touching the neighboring animal's rack, body lean and small beneath
them. Let the antlers carry the band's top third. Amber antlers,
champagne body.

**Band 3 — Woolly Rhinoceros:**
Subject: woolly rhinoceros procession. Low-slung heavy body, two
horns (long front sweep, short second), humped shoulders, shaggy
belly fringe. Reference the Chauvet rhino outlines: heavy front,
economical line. Champagne body, ivory horns.

**Band 4 — Cave Lion:**
Subject: Panthera spelaea procession, after the Chauvet lion panel:
maneless, long-bodied, round ear, intent forward stare conveyed by
posture not face detail. Stalking walk, slightly lowered head.
Ivory body, amber shading row.

**Band 5 — Steppe Bison:**
Subject: steppe bison procession, after the Altamira bison: massive
front-loaded hump, lowered head, short upcurved horns, beard fringe.
Champagne body, amber hump shading.

**Peerie rows (the connective tissue, run once):**
Now design 3 narrow Fair Isle "peerie" border bands, 7-9 stitches
tall, same grid, same palette, to run between the animal bands:
(1) a row of 8-point compass stars alternating with single dots,
(2) a zigzag reading as sediment strata with a dot in each valley,
(3) a row of small spread human hands alternating with dots, after
the cave-wall hand stencils. Same seamless tiling requirement.

## Selection rubric (score each candidate 1-5, keep ≥18/25)
1. Species ID at 3 meters (squint test on a phone screen)
2. Grid honesty: stitches stay on grid, no anti-aliased mush
3. Two-colors-per-row discipline held
4. Archaic register (cave-wall solemnity, zero cuteness)
5. Seamless tile (check the seam by eye at the strip edges)

## After selection (my work, not Gemini's)
Vector/pixel redraw of winning bands onto a true stitch chart →
seamless repeat assembly: peerie / mammoth / peerie / Megaloceros /
peerie / rhino / peerie / lion / peerie / bison → colorway files
(F/W 2026: obsidian ground; S/S option: ivory ground, obsidian
motifs) → print files for tee front panel + 18x24 print ("Capsule I
: : The Painted Ones") → Fourthwall mockups → Gerald review →
sample order before anything publishes.
